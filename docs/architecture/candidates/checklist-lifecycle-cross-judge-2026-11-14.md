## Recommendation

Use **B as the base**. Its separate operational aggregates preserve the distinction between an administrative delivery preparation and an after-sales work order while the actual delivery inspection, hold and review rules are unknown. This costs duplicate persistence and reporting code, but avoids asserting a common operational lifecycle prematurely. Both options can meet the parent-integrity invariant if their detailed constraints and concurrency protocols are implemented and verified.

Graft these two details from A:

1. Make the absence of approved delivery restriction coverage and hold-resolution authority an explicit activation blocker, including vehicle-wide and other-root restrictions. Require the eventual owner and every writer of a shared restriction to agree on a single lock order; a preparation-only lock cannot protect a simultaneous workshop action. A's proposed hold table is a design option, not an approved safety model.
2. Specify parent-bound evidence at the database level for both contexts: the outcome link must reference the exact scoped outcome revision and an evidence attachment tied to the same preparation or work order. Recheck accepted purpose, target, current clean scan and digest for the applicable action. B already sketches the delivery chain; take A's explicit equivalent for workshop evidence and prove both paths.

## Conditions and unresolved details

- **Lifecycle:** B's unique `(site, vehicle)` preparation rule is a proposed product restriction, not established evidence that a vehicle has only one preparation over its lifetime. Its partial unique open-run rule and the definition of `reviewed` likewise require an owner decision, including return, repeat inspection, cancellation, resale and historical reconstruction. Do not silently implement these as policy. A's `draft`, `approved` and `closed` vocabulary and acceptance/closure transitions also remain proposed; its projection explanation does not itself establish permission to use those transitions.
- **Schedule and authority:** USER-043's source text specifies routine maintenance at 10,000 km or one year, whichever comes first, plus other milestones. Its acceptance criteria establish that threshold as a review/intake reminder only, not a technical interval or an identical service package. Verify the applicable effective vehicle-specific manufacturer schedule before prescribing actual work. Neither candidate provides approved delivery-specific technical ownership, exact-model procedure, publication authority, assignment/review grants, independence rules or safety controls. Keep governed writes fail-closed and do not infer repair consent or G1–G5 decisions from PDI.
- **PDI scope:** Delivery pre-delivery PDI and work-order routine maintenance are required by the grounded user direction. A workshop-PDI context is not explicit in USER-043; present it as optional pending a scope decision and do not infer applicability or authority from the required delivery PDI.
- **Scope and historical facts:** Establish a legitimate first-site association for a vehicle already known elsewhere without leaking another site's data. Sales visibility of preparation and technical details needs an explicit reader policy. Existing observations remain attributed notes and existing deliveries remain dated facts; a new link must not assert a historical inspection or approval. Any retrospective run needs a separately approved chronology and correction policy.
- **Foreign keys and revisions:** B's application-decision FK must reference an actually unique tuple, and outcome, assignment, submitted-item-set and review references must include the complete scoped parent/run/item/revision keys. Its outcome-evidence relation must carry the same preparation in both FKs. State all non-null columns and referenced unique keys in executable DDL. A similarly needs exact assignment and submission-revision FKs, the full context-plus-kind target key, and non-null scope, vehicle and discriminator columns so nullable composite FKs cannot bypass parent integrity. Verify existing schemas and migration privileges before selecting either DDL shape.
- **Concurrency and preservation:** Both designs depend on locks shared with actual Sales, identity, source, scan, grant and restriction writers; a lock named only in a new command does not serialize an existing writer. Prove lock ordering and withdrawal/revocation/hold races on PostgreSQL with the runtime role. Preserve existing IDs, snapshots, source observations and access boundaries; do not present migration success or SQLite tests as concurrency or UAT evidence.

The next design step is to resolve the preparation cardinality and shared restriction ownership, then turn B's scoped relation and lock contracts into a reviewable migration and PostgreSQL negative/race test plan. Keep operational technical writes disabled pending the relevant approved policies and grants.
