## Purpose and boundaries

A service request records a concern and follow-up before or after the vehicle arrives. It is not a work order, acceptance of custody, diagnosis, permission to repair, or a safety or payment approval. Sales staff, an advisor, or another authorized staff member may record a request on behalf of a caller. The server checks site membership and the configured permission for every read and write; a role name alone does not confer a technical grant. Owner access and outbound notifications are separate capabilities.

Service requests also carry a requested service type: repair/maintenance, routine maintenance, PSAI, or Service Centre PDI. Customer or third-party entry is request-only and does not create a work order or reveal a vehicle/customer match. The Service Advisor confirms the reported facts and converts an accepted request to one typed work order. The external Service Centre PDI is separate from later Sales delivery-preparation PDI. PDI requests are fee-bearing; exact rates and billing authority remain unconfigured, so the billable quote and commercial handover stay blocked until approved.

## Request-to-arrival handoff

When a request becomes a vehicle visit, keep its original concern, caller and relationship, phone, reported vehicle and paying client, source channel, and callback action/time visible beside the reception form. Label these as reported values; staff must confirm the payer, vehicle and visit contact in the actual intake fields. Explain that submitting creates one work order for Operations review and does not authorize diagnosis or repair. The request conversion command remains atomic and links both records. After success, take staff to the resulting work order; if a response is lost, retry the same command without creating a second visit.

## Record and history

Each request has a stable ID, organization and site, version, creation time and actor, source channel, reported concern, and optional source-reported vehicle, paying client, contact name, contact phone and contact relationship. Capture the contact's relationship to the visit without assuming that caller, owner and payer are the same. A request may be saved with incomplete details. Selected canonical parties and vehicles are explicit site-scoped references; unverified names, registration and VIN remain attributed observations, never automatic matches or merges. Show conflicts and their sources to the advisor. Do not copy historical purchaser claims into owner or payer fields. Older free-text contact observations remain preserved as legacy text and are not guessed into the structured fields.

The first save may include an optional next contact step and callback time, keeping an incoming call and its planned follow-up in one action. When a next step is supplied, assign the request to the staff member who created it; show this responsibility and allow a later reasoned reassignment. A callback time requires a next step. If there is no follow-up, leave assignment and callback fields empty. This intake plan is request state, not evidence that an outbound contact was attempted; actual attempts remain separate attributed history entries. Interpret entered callback times in the site's configured timezone, convert them to a UTC instant, and reject nonexistent or repeated local times at daylight-saving changes instead of guessing.

Contact attempts and updates are append-only entries with actor, recorded time, contact channel, outcome, concise notes and optional next action and due time. Corrections add an attributed correction referencing the earlier entry. Outcomes distinguish reached, no answer, callback requested and other recorded outcomes; an attempt must not imply the customer was contacted successfully. Keep the current follow-up owner, action and due time on the request with the history needed to explain changes. A due time is stored as an instant and displayed in the site's time zone. Requests without a due time remain visible as unscheduled, not overdue.

The inbound caller's name, phone and relationship are separate optional observations from the paying client, vehicle owner and visit contact. Preserve phone formatting; do not infer identity, consent or ownership from it. Historical free-text contact observations are retained without mapping them to a structured field unless reviewed and confirmed.

Before submitting a contact attempt or correction, store its exact payload, command ID and expected version in account-, site- and request-scoped tab storage. After a reload or lost response, offer a locked retry using that same command and payload. Do not edit or silently expire an ambiguous command; clear it only after the server confirms success or staff explicitly check history and discard it. Preserve unreadable markers and ask staff to check history before clearing them. Clear recoverable command drafts at sign-out. The API rechecks current membership and permission before replaying a receipt.

Use explicit open, converted and closed states. Only an open request that is not linked to a work order can be closed; the close command requires a reason and expected version. A closed, unlinked request can be reopened with a separate reasoned, versioned command. Both transitions write an immutable status-history row, domain event, idempotency receipt and outbox item in one transaction. The history records actor, time, prior and next status, reason and version; staff can review it through a paginated, site-scoped read. A closed request has no contact-entry form, and the queue offers a separate closed filter. Closure does not delete history. Conversion retains a link to the controlling visit and work order and preserves contact history. Further customer updates remain possible after conversion, associated with the linked job. Linked work cannot be closed through this request workflow. Reopening or correcting an erroneous closure requires an auditable command, not deletion.

## Queue and workflow

A site-scoped queue shows open requests assigned to the user and, where authorized, the site queue. Show contact name or available identifier, concern summary, assigned person, next action, due time and missing identity/client facts. Provide due, overdue, unscheduled, linked-job and closed filters, deterministic ordering and server pagination. Do not expose contact details across sites. The advisor can open a request, record an attempt, assign a follow-up and review source observations before reception. A callback is complete only when a staff member records its outcome; time passing or opening a request does not complete it.

## Conversion to reception

Conversion uses the existing reception validation and authorization. The staff member explicitly confirms the paying service client, vehicle and reception facts required by that operation. Uncertain VINs remain unresolved until corrected or confirmed from an appropriate source. Matching results are suggestions only. The operation locks the request and uses its expected version, rechecks site access, creates the visit and work order, and links them atomically with the request event, idempotency receipt and outbox item. A uniqueness constraint prevents two controlling conversions; a retry with the same command and payload returns the original result after authorization is rechecked. A different concurrent conversion or stale version returns a clear conflict and the current state. Failures leave neither a partial visit nor a converted request. Conversion does not pass any work-order gate.

If a vehicle arrives without an earlier request, reception remains available. Linking an existing request to an already-created visit requires a separate explicit, audited command with same-site validation and a one-controlling-visit rule; do not infer links from a VIN or phone number. Preserve the original request even when a later correction changes its linked facts.
