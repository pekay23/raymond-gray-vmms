# SOP-003 — Routine maintenance and inspection

**Status:** DRAFT — model-specific schedule and technical approval required\
**Revision:** 0.1 (working draft)\
**Effective date:** None\
**Process owner / approver:** Pending technical and management appointment\
**Applies to:** Service Advisor, Operations Admin / Head of Service Centre, Team Lead, Supervisor, Technician / Engineer and Parts/Stores where assigned\
**Related controls:** G1–G5; `REV-006`, `REV-007`, `REV-008`, `REV-010`, `REV-012`

> Use the controlling manufacturer's maintenance schedule for the exact vehicle model, variant, powertrain, market and service history. The 10,000 km or 12-month trigger is an owner-requested intake reminder (whichever occurs first); it is not a claim that every vehicle's required work is identical or that a manufacturer prescribes the same interval. No measurement limit, replacement item, fluid, reset, diagnostic procedure or HV work is invented here.

## 1. Purpose and trigger

Prompt the Service Advisor to consider a routine service visit when the vehicle reaches 10,000 km since its applicable baseline or 12 months since its applicable time baseline, whichever occurs first, and at any additional manufacturer milestone. The reminder prompts contact or an intake conversation; it does not itself create a booking, approve work or imply that the service is overdue. Record the last verified service date and odometer, source, current odometer and unit, and the schedule/revision used to calculate each due item. If the baseline or schedule is unknown, show due status as unknown and ask for review.

## 2. Intake and planning

1. Verify vehicle identity against the physical vehicle and documents. Record the paying client, reported owner and visit contact separately.
2. Confirm customer concerns and requested scope; obtain purpose-specific consent for checks, diagnostics, road test, data access and proposed work. Record customer-supplied history as reported, not verified.
3. Select the exact effective model/variant/powertrain schedule and service history. Create one task for each applicable prescribed activity, with source section/revision, due basis, required competence, tools/parts, evidence and review needs. Keep manufacturer tasks separate from local inspection items and customer concerns.
4. Operations reviews the job and assigns an eligible Team Lead. The Team Lead prepares the plan and hands work to a Supervisor; the Supervisor assigns eligible technicians/engineers.
5. If the model-specific schedule, baseline, procedure, authority, equipment, part, customer approval or safety evidence is missing, flag the task blocked and request the missing input. Do not substitute a generic 10,000 km service list.

## 3. Maintenance task record

For each task, record its category, exact approved procedure and revision, due date/odometer and source, scope, assigned accountable worker, acknowledgement, start/finish status, actual measurements/units where required, findings, actions, parts and traceability if used, evidence, customer approval where applicable, unresolved reason, reviewer and timestamps. Allowed outcomes are `completed`, `failed`, `not completed`, `blocked`, `not applicable` and `not checked`. Require an explanation for every outcome other than completed. Corrections append and preserve history.

## 4. Form/checklist sections

| Section | Questions/fields | Completion control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visit and vehicle identity | Booking, client, visit contact, reported owner, verified VIN/chassis, model/variant, powertrain, current odometer/unit | Mismatch or unknown odometer remains visible; do not infer identity |
| Service due calculation | Previous verified service date/odometer, next time/mileage milestone, schedule source/revision, due calculation and reviewer | Unknown baselines or schedule produce `unknown`, not a fabricated due state |
| Customer-reported concerns | Concern, source, when/how it occurs, consent, callback need | Preserve reported wording separately from technician finding |
| Scheduled work | One line per current OEM-prescribed task, interval basis, steps reference, assigned person, result, values/units, parts, evidence and comments | No task is executable without an effective applicable procedure and eligible authority |
| General condition observations | Body/glass, lights, tyres/wheels, cabin, visible leaks/condition, displays/warnings, charging/fuel interface, brakes/suspension only as specified by the approved check | Use approved criteria and safe methods; record observations without diagnosing beyond authority |
| Road test / diagnostic work | Consent, approved route/setup, authorized test/procedure, performer, conditions, result and evidence | Separate consent and role/qualification required; do not imply consent from intake |
| Additional work | Proposed scope, estimate basis, required client/payer approval, decision evidence, reason for declined/deferred scope | Do not begin out-of-scope work without the required approval |
| Parts and materials | Approved part identity, source, lot/serial if applicable, compatibility, custody and unused/returned disposition | Follow current inventory and traceability procedure; no undocumented substitution |
| Closeout and next service | Per-task status and reason, holds, unresolved items, next milestone/source, Supervisor completion review, Team Lead release, Head inspection | Overall report cannot be complete while required task/evidence/sign-off is missing |

This section list is a configurable form skeleton, not a universal set of required procedures. Technical owners must map actual model-specific tasks and applicability before an effective template is released.

## 5. Review, report and communication

The Supervisor reviews each assigned task for evidence and records the technical completion decision. The Team Lead reviews the full work plan, unresolved items and required independent checks; records final release and collection-zone placement only when permitted. The Head of Service Centre records the separate inspection/sign-off. The Service Advisor receives the completed review state, prepares the invoice and communicates the work performed, work not completed and reason, observations, parts if approved for disclosure, open recommendations and next due basis. The report must preserve distinctions between requested, approved, performed, not performed and unknown work.

## 6. Approval required before use

Appoint a technical schedule owner and approvers; load the current manufacturer schedule and effective service information for every model/variant; reconcile market-specific intervals, special campaigns, warranty instructions, inspection values, tool/calibration needs, role qualifications, evidence and independent checks; approve customer-facing wording and template revisions; and train/acknowledge affected roles. Until then the form is a draft framework. It cannot authorize technical work or represent a completed maintenance schedule.
